A toy network, a stuck puck, and a score that is not real

Kerem Zengin · 4 November 2026

Mock. The controls change the pictures. Nothing on this page was measured, trained, or claimed as a result.

TL;DR

This page uses the same kinds of controls as Sparks of Weight-Space Programming: a Read / Fix / Improve teaser, a Setup drawer, and figures that change when you press a button. The writing, the pictures, and the numbers are a mock. They are not that post’s results.

The teaser’s list of numbers is a file. The puck follows a path written in the script. The score is the formula \(s(n)=12+29(1-e^{-n/18})\). Dragging the slider evaluates that formula. It does not train anything.

What you can click

Five widgets follow. Each one keeps a control and a picture in step.

  1. Switch a picture. Ring, Steps, and Cross are three formulas on a 12×12 grid.
  2. Play a path. One path hits the block. The other goes around it.
  3. Drag a score. The slider, the chart, and the table share one expression.
  4. Switch a grid. Sparse, Single, and Other paint different cells.
  5. Switch two bars. Question and Guess move the same two shares.

Three pictures, one switch

Press a name. The grid redraws from that formula, and the note beside it changes to the matching sentence. The sentence is stored next to the button. It is not inferred from the pixels, though the pixels do show the shape the sentence describes.

A puck that takes the path you pick

Stuck sends the puck right until it meets the block. Edited sends it up, across, and down to the green dot. Press Play to run the chosen path once. Pressing Stuck or Edited again puts the puck back at the start.

One formula, three places

The score is

\[ s(n)=12+29\left(1-e^{-n/18}\right), \]

with \(n\) from 0 to 50. The slider evaluates it. The curve is the same expression. The table is four samples of it, stored in data/budget.json, and a row lights up when the slider sits on that sample. The word in the last column is there so the table cannot be mistaken for a benchmark.

A grid you can point at

Eight cells are painted. The rest are empty. Point at a cell, or focus it from the keyboard, and the line under the grid names the row and the column.

A grid that follows the button

The reference post switches a small grid between three cases. This one does the same job with made-up marks. Press a case. The painted cells and the sentence change together. Nothing was measured.

Two bars, one choice

The reference post also sets two shares side by side and lets the reader compare them. Here the shares are a formula of the button. “Question” puts most of the bar on the left. “Guess” puts most of it on the right.

A note on the numbers

The pictures redraw, and the slider matches the formula. The story around them is made up. Nothing here was measured or trained. The post this layout follows is Takehi, Teoh, Zhang, and Khattab, November 2026.
