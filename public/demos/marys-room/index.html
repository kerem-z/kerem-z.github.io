What Mary had not seen

Kerem Zengin · 4 November 2026

Draft. Frank Jackson’s thought experiment, drawn small enough to click. The argument is contested. This page shows the step people argue about, and one famous objection.

TL;DR

Mary knows every physical fact about color and has never seen a color. Then she sees red. Jackson says she learns something. If that something is a fact, then the physical list was not the whole list.

Open the door

The notes stay. Opening the door adds one line, and it is not written in the same ink. Jackson’s claim is that the new line is knowledge she lacked, so knowing all the physical facts is not knowing everything. The papers are “Epiphenomenal Qualia” (1982) and “What Mary Didn’t Know” (1986).

Or she learned a skill

The ability reply, associated with Laurence Nemirow and David Lewis, says Mary does not learn a new fact. She learns how to see, remember, and imagine red. A skill can be new without the list of facts being incomplete. The button below swaps the gloss on the line that appeared. The notes do not change.
